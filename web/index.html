chela explained

chela splits a BIP-39 wallet seed (or any short secret) into N shares using Shamir's Secret Sharing, then recovers the original from any M of those shares. Each share is a printable card: a short BIP-39 word list you can lock in a safe, hand to a trusted person, or store in a different country. The scheme is information-theoretically secure — any M−1 shares reveal nothing about the secret — making it practical for inheritance planning and disaster recovery without any single point of loss or compromise.

Who chela is for

  • Inheritance planning. Split a hardware-wallet seed so heirs can recover access without a single point of failure, and without any one executor holding the whole secret before it is needed.
  • Disaster recovery. Store shares in physically separate locations (home safe, bank box, trusted relative). Losing one location does not lose the secret; a house fire or flood cannot wipe out recovery.
  • Eliminating the single-point-of-compromise. No single safe, hard drive, or person holds a copy of the whole secret. An adversary who obtains one card learns nothing.

Who chela is not for

  • It does not replace a hardware wallet. chela protects the backup seed; your hardware wallet protects day-to-day signing. Use both.
  • It does not hide secrets from yourself. Recovery requires M shares in the same room. If you lock all shares in inaccessible places, you lock yourself out too.
  • It does not defend against a coalition of M cardholders. If M people you gave shares to conspire, they can recover the secret. Choose your cardholders with the same care you would choose co-signers.
